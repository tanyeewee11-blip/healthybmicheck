Healthy BMI for Women: An Age-Specific Guide

Last updated: May 2025  ·  7 min read  ·  Medically Reviewed

While the BMI formula is universal, a healthy range for women involves unique biological considerations. Factors like higher essential fat requirements, hormonal changes, and age-related bone density loss all play a role in how women should interpret their Body Mass Index.

What Is a Healthy BMI for Women?

The standard WHO healthy BMI range for adult women is 18.5 to 24.9. However, clinical evidence suggests that this "one-size-fits-all" number may not be ideal for every stage of life or every ethnicity.

Note for Asian Women: Many health organizations recommend a lower overweight threshold of BMI 23.0 for women of South or East Asian heritage due to higher visceral fat risks.

Why Women Naturally Have Higher Body Fat

Women are biologically programmed to carry more essential fat than men to support reproductive functions and oestrogen production. A healthy woman may have 21–33% body fat, whereas a healthy man might have 8–24%, even if they share the same BMI.

How Hormones Affect Your BMI

Menopause and Body Composition

During menopause, declining oestrogen often leads to fat redistribution from the hips and thighs to the abdomen. This can increase health risks even if your total BMI remains stable. During this time, muscle preservation through strength training is crucial.

Waist Circumference: The Vital Companion

BMI doesn't tell us where fat is stored. For women, a waist measurement over 80cm (31.5 inches) indicates an increased risk of chronic disease, regardless of your BMI score.

Important: BMI is a screening tool, not a diagnostic one. Always consult your doctor for a personalized health assessment.
