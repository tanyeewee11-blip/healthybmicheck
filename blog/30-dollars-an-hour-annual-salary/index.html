$30 an Hour is How Much a Year?

Salary GuideJune 20268 min read

$30/hour × 2,080 hours = $62,400/year before taxes. After federal income tax and FICA, a single filer in a no-state-tax state takes home approximately $50,800/year ($4,233/month). This guide covers every pay period, after-tax take-home by state, a realistic monthly budget, and the fastest paths from $30/hr to $40+/hr.

Is $30 an Hour a Good Wage in 2026?

$30/hr ($62,400/year) is above the US individual median income — roughly top 35% of individual earners. In practical terms:

  • Comfortable: Most Midwest and Southern markets — Dallas, Houston, Phoenix, Columbus, Indianapolis, Kansas City, Charlotte. Homeownership is achievable with planning; savings of 10–15% are realistic.
  • Livable with margin: Mid-tier metros like Denver, Austin, Nashville, Portland, Atlanta. Renting is comfortable; buying requires careful planning.
  • Tight: High-cost metros — San Francisco, NYC, Seattle, Boston. Doable with roommates or a long commute but limited savings capacity.

How to Move from $30 to $40+/Hour

  • Specialty credential in your field: At $30/hr, a targeted certification typically unlocks $38–$48/hr within 12–24 months — master electrician license, RN-to-BSN, AWS Solutions Architect, or CPA for accounting roles.
  • Move to a higher-paying employer: The same role often pays $30/hr at a small employer and $40/hr at a large hospital system, tech company, or government agency. Job switching at this income yields 15–25% increases on average.
  • Add management responsibility: Moving from individual contributor to team lead or supervisor typically adds $4–$8/hr without requiring new credentials.
  • Geographic arbitrage: Remote workers earning $30/hr in a high-cost city can maintain the same income while moving to a lower-cost market — effectively a 20–35% purchasing power increase.
💡 At $62,400/year: You're in the 22% federal bracket for most of your income. Pre-tax 401(k) contributions save you $0.22 per dollar contributed — every $2,000 to a traditional 401(k) saves $440 in federal tax immediately. If your employer matches, capturing the full match is the single highest-return financial move available at this income level. After the match, consider a Roth IRA — at $62,400, you're still well below the $150,000 phase-out threshold for direct contributions.
